## First phone session

1. Use a rear-camera Android phone with torch. Record anonymous device ID, manufacturer/model, API/ABI, build hash, 30/60-second mode, nominal hardware facts and observed selected stream/cadence in [`../device-matrix.md`](../device-matrix.md). The debug build permits an engineering capture without an account and does not save that anonymous result.
2. Grant camera permission, select activity and consent, then try a steady finger placement. Observe whether torch comes on, the transient graph follows the actual camera signal, quality reaches the measuring state, and a completed result appears. Record attempt count, valid seconds, duration, SQI score/issues, HR, PRV availability and the compatibility classification. A 60-second accepted debug capture may also show a clearly marked **research** respiratory candidate and **unitless** red/green optical ratio. Record the method version and whether each candidate was withheld; never call the ratio SpO₂ percent.
3. Repeat with deliberate bad contact, movement, darkness/torch interruption, cancel, background, retry, timeout and normal finish. For each attempt, record whether an old/provisional number or trend survives rejection and whether camera/torch shuts off on exits. Stop and report a number after failed SQI, a lingering torch, or a crash as a release blocker.
4. Inspect profile compatibility and history after an accepted account-backed capture and after a rejected one. Rejected sessions must not create a numerical history/trend point. Confirm respiratory rate, SpO₂ and blood pressure remain unavailable in the ordinary result, history and PDF; ECG Insight and overall-risk category remain unavailable.

## Paired reference session

Before collecting final comparison data, freeze the protocol, instrument identity/accuracy, time alignment, acceptance thresholds and development versus held-out split in [`validation-plan.md`](validation-plan.md). For HR, use a time-aligned ECG reference where possible. For PRV RMSSD/SDNN, retain ECG beat intervals, not merely displayed BPM. For respiratory rate, use a belt/capnography reference, with manual counts labelled as a limited pilot. For SpO₂, use an appropriate reference oximeter and record the **observed** saturation range; normal-range volunteers do not validate low-range labels. For blood pressure, use a validated cuff and separate calibration from evaluation, including change/drift conditions. Model ECG timing requires synchronized phone-camera PPG and ECG, rights, calibrated confidence and an independent subject split.

For every metric, keep accepted and rejected attempts in the denominator. Report participant count separately from attempt count; error/bias/limits of agreement by device and condition; missing results and retries; and any software defect. Never put participant identifiers, raw frames, raw PPG, ECG traces, credentials or clinical source records in Git. Give engineering the anonymous build/device/condition, steps, expected versus observed behavior, and aggregate evidence so the exact failing gate can be reproduced. A changed camera, threshold, pipeline or model version requires affected re-evaluation.

## Engineering response and open external decisions

Engineering owns reproduction, software fixes, regression tests and evidence updates after the field findings arrive. F10 cannot be enabled from the present research checkpoint: it lacks a calibrated confidence output, phone-domain ECG agreement and distribution decision. F11 and F12 have debug research candidates only; their production values require method-specific reference error/yield and supported-range decisions. F13 needs a defensible single-camera calibration method and cuff study before an estimator can be justified. F14's four overall-risk levels and clinical alerts need an expert-reviewed rule set and false-alert/comprehension evidence. Until those inputs exist, their unavailable states are unfinished requirements, not completed features.
